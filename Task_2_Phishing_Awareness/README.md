# 🎣 Task 2 — Phishing Awareness Training

Part of the **[CodeAlpha Cyber Security Internship](../README.md)**.

An educational presentation and reference kit designed to teach employees, students, or teams how to **recognize, avoid, and report phishing attacks** — one of the most common and effective attack vectors in cybersecurity today.

---

## 🎯 Objective

- Understand what phishing is and the psychology attackers exploit
- Learn to identify the six major types of phishing attacks
- Practice spotting red flags using a realistic (fictional) example
- Know exactly what to do when a phishing attempt is suspected
- Adopt practical habits that prevent successful attacks

## 🗂️ Presentation Outline

1. **Title** — Phishing Awareness Training
2. **Agenda** — what the training covers
3. **What Is Phishing?** — definition + the psychology behind why it works (urgency, authority, fear, curiosity)
4. **Types of Attacks** — Email phishing, spear phishing, whaling, smishing, vishing, clone phishing
5. **Anatomy of an Attack** — a fully annotated, fictional phishing email broken down flag-by-flag
6. **Impact & Statistics** — why phishing remains the #1 breach vector
7. **Spotting Red Flags** — an 8-point practical checklist
8. **Response Plan** — a 5-step process for what to do when you suspect phishing
9. **Prevention** — best practices (MFA, password managers, patching, training, filtering, reporting culture)
10. **Key Takeaways** — summary of the core lessons
11. **Closing** — thank you / call to action

---

## 🧠 Key Concepts Covered

- **Social engineering** — attacks exploit human psychology, not just software vulnerabilities
- **Email header analysis** — verifying the true sender domain vs. the display name
- **URL inspection** — hovering to preview a link's real destination before clicking
- **Multi-factor authentication (MFA)** — why it blunts the impact of a stolen password
- **Incident response** — the correct sequence: don't click → inspect → report → delete → reset credentials if needed

---

## ⚖️ Notes on the Examples Used

All phishing examples in this deck and in `mock_phishing_examples.md` are **entirely fictional** — no real company, brand, or individual is impersonated. They exist purely to illustrate common phishing patterns for educational purposes.
