# 🚩 Phishing Red Flags — Quick Reference Checklist

Keep this checklist handy. If a message you receive raises **two or more** of these flags, treat it as suspicious and verify before acting.

## ✉️ Sender & Header
- [ ] The sender's **display name** looks legitimate, but the actual **email address/domain** doesn't match the organization
- [ ] The domain is a look-alike (e.g. extra letters, hyphens, or a different top-level domain than the real one)
- [ ] You weren't expecting this message, and the sender is someone you rarely (or never) hear from

## ⏱️ Tone & Urgency
- [ ] The message creates a false sense of **urgency** ("act within 24 hours", "immediate action required")
- [ ] It threatens a negative consequence (account suspension, legal action, fees) if you don't respond quickly
- [ ] It appeals to curiosity or reward ("You've won...", "Unusual activity detected")

## 🔗 Links & Attachments
- [ ] Hovering over a link shows a destination URL that doesn't match the link text or the real organization
- [ ] The link uses a shortened URL (bit.ly, tinyurl, etc.) hiding the true destination
- [ ] There's an unexpected attachment, especially `.zip`, `.exe`, `.js`, or a macro-enabled Office file

## 🗣️ Content & Language
- [ ] The greeting is generic ("Dear Customer", "Dear User") instead of your actual name
- [ ] There are spelling mistakes, awkward grammar, or inconsistent formatting
- [ ] The message asks you to confirm or provide a password, PIN, or full card number directly

## 🌐 Request Type
- [ ] You're asked to log in via a link instead of navigating to the site yourself
- [ ] You're asked to make an urgent payment, wire transfer, or purchase gift cards
- [ ] A "colleague" or "executive" is asking for something unusual over email/chat/text, especially involving money or credentials

---

## ✅ If You Spot a Red Flag

1. **Don't click** any links or open any attachments.
2. **Don't reply** to the sender.
3. **Verify independently** — contact the organization or person through a known, trusted channel (not the one in the suspicious message).
4. **Report** it to your IT/Security team or use your organization's "Report Phishing" button.
5. **Delete** the message once it's reported.
6. If you already clicked a link or entered credentials, **change your password immediately** and notify IT/Security.

> **Golden rule:** When in doubt, don't click — verify through a separate, trusted channel first.
